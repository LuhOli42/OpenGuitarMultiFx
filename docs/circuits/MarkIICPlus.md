# Mark IIC+-Style Amplifier

A component-level model of the **Mesa/Boogie Mark IIC+** Lead channel, built from a DIY Fever clone schematic
(by Bancika, diy-fever.com — the closest available component-level reference for this amp). The Mark IIC+ is
arguably the most revered high-gain amplifier ever built, defining the modern "Mesa" lead tone heard on
countless metal and progressive rock recordings from the mid-1980s onward.

Display name: **Mark IIC+-Style Amplifier** (trademark-safe convention).

## Architecture

**Lead channel only** is modelled, as the amp's defining voice. The Clean and Rhythm channels are not
implemented.

### Preamp (five cascaded 12AX7 gain stages)

1. **V1a** (150K plate from B+4/sF, 68K grid stopper, 1M grid leak, 1.5K/0.47µF bypassed cathode)
   - The input stage. Guitar signal enters through a 68K grid stopper.

2. **1nF treble-forward coupling** → **Gain pot** (1MA audio taper, split rGainTop/rGainBot)
   - THE defining voicing element of the Mark series: the tiny 1nF cap rolls off bass before the Gain pot,
     so the high-gain stages amplify mostly upper harmonics. This is what gives the Mark its tight, focused
     lead tone vs. the mushier bass of a Marshall-derived design.

3. **V1b** (100K plate from B+3/sE, 100K series resistor from Gain wiper, 3.3M grid leak, 1.5K/6.8µF cathode)
   - Second gain stage. The 100K series from the wiper adds isolation.

4. **V2a** (82K plate from B+3/sE, 470K grid leak, 120pF enhanced Miller, 1.5K/2.2µF cathode)
   - Third gain stage. 47nF coupling from V1b through a 680K series resistor.

5. **68K/3.3K voltage divider** (attenuates V2a signal to ~4.6%)
   - A deliberate, massive attenuation between V2a and V2b. The Mark's secret: the preamp has FAR more
     gain than any single stage needs, and this divider throws most of it away, keeping only the harmonics
     generated by V2a's clipping.

6. **V2b** (270K plate from B+3/sE, 470K grid leak, 250pF enhanced Miller, 87K unbypassed cathode)
   - Fourth gain stage. Very low current (~25µA), very low gain (~2.5x), plate near supply (~415V).
     The 87K unbypassed cathode makes this a very soft, gradual compression stage.

7. **547pF silver mica coupling** → **V3a** (100K plate from B+2/sD, 220K grid leak, 1.5K unbypassed cathode)
   - Fifth and final gain stage. The 547pF silver mica cap is a high-pass at ~1.3kHz with the 220K grid leak,
     further tightening the bass before the tone stack.

8. **V3b cathode follower** (via `addFollower`)
   - Buffer between the preamp and tone stack. DC offset measured in a two-pass build.

### Tone stack

Fender-derived TMB (treble/mid/bass) with Mark-specific values:
- 47K slope resistor
- 250pF treble cap (vs SLO-100's 470pF — brighter)
- 22nF bass and mid caps
- Treble 250K, Mid 25K, Bass 250K linear pots

### Phase inverter

12AX7 long-tailed-pair, same topology as the SLO-100:
- 82K/81K plate loads, 470Ω tail resistor
- 47pF compensation cap between plates
- 22nF coupling caps to the power tubes

### Power amp

4 x 6L6GC beam tetrodes as two push-pull pairs (fixed bias at -55V):
- 0.047µF coupling caps, 1.1K grid stoppers, 220K grid leak resistors
- Same topology as the SLO-100

### Output transformer and global feedback

- 150K feedback resistor (vs SLO-100's 175K — more aggressive feedback for the lead channel)
- 25K presence pot, 0.1µF feedback cap, 1.5nF stray capacitance

### Power supply

470V nominal (vs SLO-100's 497V). Solid-state rectifier, 60Ω series resistance.
Choke-filtered chain: sA (plates) → sB (screens) → 10K → sC (PI/B+1, 16µF) → 10K → sD (B+2/V3, 10µF) →
1K → sE (B+3/V1b/V2, 10µF) → 1K → sF (B+4/V1a, 10µF).

## Open issues

Same kg1 instability pattern as the SLO-100 and JCM800: the full reference model's absolute gain is collapsed
by the 40x kg1 stability fix on both PI triode and power pentode. The reduced-order power stage keeps the
Twin Reverb's own fitted 6L6GC constants (same tube type, same push-pull topology) rather than calibrating
against this unreliable reference.

## Performance

- Full model plucked note: 0 failures, 0 recoveries (but near-silent output due to kg1 collapse)
- Reduced-order: ~14.7% avg CPU, ~20.5% worst block (5 preamp gain stages — same cost class as the SLO-100)
- Unity trim: -0.51 dB
